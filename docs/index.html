How well can you really forecast suburb rents?

Twenty-six candidate models, 862 rental series, 146 suburb groups and one rule: every model is scored the way the product actually runs — forecasting eight quarters ahead while eating its own predictions. Baselines are allowed to win. This page is the short version; the full paper has the maths.

What this model does not do

  • It forecasts group medians, not individual properties. The bands are not the range of individual rents.
  • It does not prove linear models beat gradient boosting in general — only in this grid, on this target, under this protocol.
  • It explains nothing causally. Postcode and bedroom effects are associations inside a pipeline.
  • It has no audited accuracy beyond three quarters ahead.
  • It assumes the measurement process is stable; a change in bond-lodgement coverage or the moving-annual definition would break it silently.
